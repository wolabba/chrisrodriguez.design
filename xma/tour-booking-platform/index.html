Context

Local tours, global storefront — and 30% gone with every sale

XMA's founder identified there was a lack of local expert support for travelers who booked a tour through a major OTA — GetYourGuide, Viator — and that no local platform existed to provide it either.

With no platform of its own, XMA — like every other local guide and agency in Manuel Antonio — depended entirely on the major OTAs to sell online, paying a 30% fee on every booking.

The problem

Two symptoms. One cause.

Travelers (my users) struggled with a lack of local platforms online — so they overpaid on the major OTAs, which sell reach, not local knowledge or support when something goes wrong.

The business lost 30% of every sale — its self-made website couldn't handle bookings or take payments. That only reinforced its dependency on the OTAs.

The challenge

Live today, final tomorrow

The business had a hard requirement: build a functional website, live and converting, while research for the final design was still underway.

"How do I ship a converting site now — while I run the research for the final design?"
Approach

Evidence first — assumptions never.

01

Diagnose the drop-off.

I audited real sessions on the existing site to see exactly what was pushing travelers away.

02

Ship the pilot.

The findings from the diagnose let me launch a functional, converting site fast — meeting the business's requirement and proving the concept early.

03

Find the real need.

With the site live and converting, I ran a design-thinking loop — empathize, define, ideate — to lay the groundwork for a future final design based on evidence, not guesswork.

Discover

Ten sessions told me what a survey never would

I ran a Hotjar watch party across ten high-relevance session recordings, chosen to pinpoint what was quietly killing functionality and conversion on the client's self-made site.

Design thinking

They didn't want a better tour. They wanted a local friend.

I interviewed 4 frequent travelers and tour-takers to understand their needs, frustrations, and — above all — what they actually wanted from a local platform: not just a way to book a tour, but the kind of trustworthy, on-the-ground guidance you'd get from a friend who already lives there.

From there, I synthesized that research into empathy maps, cross-referencing interests against pain points until five clear patterns emerged — the patterns that shaped the user personas I'd use to ideate solutions.

Reflection

Adapt the process — don't abandon it

The real lesson here wasn't about method — it was about priorities. A textbook design process rarely survives a client's reality: XMA needed a live, converting pilot site, and the business couldn't wait for a full research cycle to finish.
